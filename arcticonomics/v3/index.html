I · The law

The Arctic economy runs counter-cyclical to the world.

Value depends on how much of something exists, how many want it, and what they can pay. In a world of trust and free trade, the cheapest barrel wins, and the Arctic, cold, remote and costly, is not needed. When the world breaks, it becomes vital, whatever the cost.

Every time a major producer falls into war, revolution or sanctions, Norwegian oil becomes one of the few trusted sources left. Every time all producers pump freely, prices sink below the waterline. Norway’s welfare state rests, in part, on other people’s chaos.

The world around it has.

Age of the crust. Cratons this old keep their minerals pure and concentrated.

About $1.8 trillion of known resources at October 2025 prices, by a rough FRONTTIER estimate, and most of the island is still unexplored. Eighty per cent of it lies under ice. In a calm world, it is too far and too costly. In a divided one, every power wants it as a partner.

V · The great Nordic paradox

The Nordics are only competitive when they are the exception, not the rule.

  1. Democratic in a world that turns autocratic.
  2. Stable in a world that turns chaotic.
  3. United in a world that turns fragmented.

If the Nordics made the whole world as peaceful as they are, they would erase the very conditions of their success. That is not a scandal. It is a story that no longer matches the facts, and the world is starting to notice.
